     1→# QuickQuid Buyer AI Readiness and Account Onboarding
     2→
     3→Use this prompt to improve the existing QuickQuid prototype.
     4→
     5→## Objective
     6→
     7→Fix the public project-readiness journey without redesigning or rebuilding the rest of the product.
     8→
     9→The current problem is critical: an unauthenticated visitor can type into **“What needs to get finished?”**, click **“Check project readiness”**, and enter Buyer product screens without completing account creation and Buyer onboarding.
    10→
    11→Replace that behavior with a smooth, deterministic Buyer–AI readiness conversation. Let the visitor receive useful project guidance before asking them to create an account. Require account creation and minimal Buyer onboarding before the project can be saved or any Buyer dashboard can be opened.
    12→
    13→This is a prototype. Do not build a real LLM, authentication service, payment system, KYC service or escrow system. Use realistic hard-coded responses and local prototype state.
    14→
    15→## Product Rule
    16→
    17→> A guest may explore and prepare a project. Only an onboarded Buyer may save it, publish it or enter the Buyer product.
    18→
    19→Never assign a Buyer role, create a Buyer session or show the Buyer dashboard merely because a visitor clicked the readiness CTA.
    20→
    21→## Required Journey
    22→
    23→```mermaid
    24→flowchart LR
    25→    Landing["Landing page"] --> Input["Visitor enters rough request"]
    26→    Input --> Chat["Guest AI readiness conversation"]
    27→    Chat --> Ready{"Seven readiness areas complete?"}
    28→    Ready -- "No" --> Chat
    29→    Ready -- "Yes" --> Summary["Ready project summary"]
    30→    Summary --> Auth["Create account or sign in"]
    31→    Auth --> Buyer["Minimal Buyer onboarding"]
    32→    Buyer --> Restore["Restore prepared project"]
    33→    Restore --> Draft["Buyer reviews and saves draft"]
    34→    Draft --> Workspace["Project workspace"]
    35→    Workspace --> Dashboard["Buyer dashboard"]
    36→```
    37→
    38→The Buyer dashboard is the end of onboarding, not the substitute for onboarding.
    39→
    40→## Route and State Guards
    41→
    42→Use these prototype states even if the application remains a single-page experience:
    43→
    44→1. `PUBLIC_LANDING`
    45→2. `GUEST_READINESS_CHAT`
    46→3. `READINESS_SUMMARY`
    47→4. `AUTH_REQUIRED`
    48→5. `BUYER_ONBOARDING`
    49→6. `PROJECT_DRAFT_RESTORED`
    50→7. `BUYER_PROJECT`
    51→8. `BUYER_DASHBOARD`
    52→
    53→The guard for Buyer product screens is:
    54→
    55→```text
    56→isAuthenticated === true
    57→AND activeRole === "BUYER"
    58→AND buyerProfileComplete === true
    59→```
    60→
    61→If any condition is false, preserve the prepared project and send the user to the missing step. Do not silently create a Buyer identity.

   118→### Conversation style
   119→
   120→- Ask one clear question at a time.
   121→- Confirm what was understood before asking the next question.
   122→- Use short answer chips where helpful, while always permitting typed input.
   123→- Never claim that AI has approved the project, selected a Pro or guaranteed a delivery date.
   124→- Call the output **Prepared for QuickQuid review**, not automatically approved.
   125→- Show why a missing answer matters in one sentence.
   126→- Do not turn the experience into a long multi-section form.

   128→## Seven Readiness Areas
   129→
   130→The live panel must show these seven areas. A project is prepared only when all seven contain an answer.
   131→
   132→| Area | Information captured | Why QuickQuid needs it |
   133→|---|---|---|
   134→| Outcome and category | Desired result, inferred category and project archetype | Makes the request understandable and matchable |
   135→| Scope | Deliverables and explicit exclusions | Prevents hidden or moving work |
   136→| Inputs and dependencies | Existing materials, missing inputs, access and ownership | Identifies whether work can start |
   137→| Budget | Professional-fee range and flexibility | Prevents unfinanceable matching |
   138→| Timeline | Target date, reason and hard dependencies | Supports a realistic Commit Date later |
   139→| Decision and feedback | Decision-maker and expected review window | Makes Buyer responsibility explicit |
   140→| Acceptance | Objective completion and handoff criteria | Establishes what “done” means |
   141→
   142→Use clear status labels:
   143→
   144→- **Missing**
   145→- **Answered**
   146→- **Needs confirmation**
   147→- **Prepared for review**
   148→
   149→Do not use a false AI confidence percentage.

   164→
   165→- **Design only**
   166→- **Frontend build only**

   173→
   174→AI response:
   175→
   176→> I’ll treat this as a combined UI/UX and frontend project. What must be handed over at the end?

   178→Preselected editable suggestions:
   179→
   180→- Responsive landing-page design
   181→- Desktop, tablet and mobile implementation
   182→- Editable design source
   183→- Source repository
   184→- Deployed preview
   185→- Basic browser and responsive checks

   189→> Is anything explicitly outside this project?

   191→Suggested answer:
   192→
   193→> No backend, CMS, SEO campaign, copywriting or ongoing hosting support.
